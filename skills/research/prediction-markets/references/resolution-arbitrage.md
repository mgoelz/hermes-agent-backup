A whale forced a YES resolution via the ambiguous clause 'announcement without enactment' (two dictionary senses). >$500k reallocated NO→YES. An identical market closing 3 days earlier had resolved NO un-contested. (mr.ozi Substack chronology.)
3. **MicroStrategy BTC sale** (Polymarket): sale provably occurred on-chain, but criteria required confirmation *within the market timeframe*; MSTR confirmed 2 days late → NO. Time-window traps exclude events that objectively happened.
4. **Manifold 'Tesla more fully autonomous rides than Waymo 2026'** (~p=0.04, vol>1M): criteria define 'fully autonomous' strictly (no human at controls, no monitor aboard, non-trivial distance, no fixed track). Definition ≈ the whole edge; headline readers get run over.

6. **Manifold 'Did COVID come from a lab?'** (p≈0.29): resolves only at '98% definitive answer… many years after'. Open-ended capital lockup not reflected in the price.

11. **Manifold 'Will AI solve a Millennium Prize Problem before 2030'** (p≈77¢, close 2030): criteria need a PEER-REVIEWED paper or accepted CMI submission proving an autonomous-AI proof — stricter than a mere announcement, but NOT subject to the 2-year CMI crown window (peer review suffices). Lesson: near-identical titles can bind to materially different resolution standards — always diff the criteria text market-by-market before assuming cluster-wide edge; also note the irony that our own fade-YES here fights the criteria, so take the TP when it fires.
12. **Manifold 'AI solves MP in 2026' family** (18%/7%): criteria bind only to 'substantial work by AI' + announcement — a live announcement alone could resolve YES. Do NOT short these on criteria grounds during an active news cycle; the '98% CMI certainty' logic of the main market does NOT transfer. Same headline ≠ same fine print, in BOTH directions.
13. **News-shock stress test (the OpenAI Navier-Stokes announcement, 2026-09)**: the whole cluster exploded — main market 69→80¢→94¢→97¢+, '2026' family 25→56¢, '2030' variant 77→97¢. Response playbook that worked: (a) web-search the ACTUAL facts first — OpenAI's claim used a 'smooth external force' route most mathematicians exclude; Clay did NOT accept it; N-S stayed listed unsolved; formal recognition 'takes years if at all' — so the main-market criteria thesis was STABILIZED by the news even as price moved against it (hold); (b) the '2026' family binds to mere *announcement*, and an announcement HAD happened → exit immediately; selling 2502 NO-shares realized +M$76 on M$50 (exit beats thesis when the market's own criteria say the event may have occurred); (c) the '2030' variant kept a real NO case (peer review + autonomy still unlikely) but was held with an exit trigger (sell if price <80¢) instead of doubling down. Rule: when a news shock moves a whole criteria-arb cluster, re-read EACH market's criteria against the new facts and grade every position exit/hold/double — the headline moves all prices together, but the fine print separates them. Also: the deciding fact for institution-anchored markets (does the institution accept the claim?) comes from the institution's own site / reputable coverage — never from the announcing lab's press release.
14. **Main-market holding through a hype blowout — the hard test**: the main Millennium position went to −69 M$ unrealized (price 36¢→80¢→97¢) with zero news *on the market itself*; the move was hype bleeding over from sister markets. Held on the criteria logic (CMI acceptance provably impossible before the deadline), with the exit plan pinned to an external event (the creator's answer to the scheduled criteria-clarification question), not to pain level. Corollary: avoid holding OTHER positions whose thesis depends on the same hype dying — the fade-YES position on the sister market was profiting from the very hype hurting the main position; recognize when your own book is internally fighting the same news flow, and size so the net cluster exposure matches your actual conviction.
15. **NO-side limits on hot markets — execution quirk that paid**: the straddle's `NO @ limitProb 0.94` on a market where NO traded at ~2-3¢ filled instantly at ~2¢/share — 2502 shares for M$25. As intended it was a nonsense price (NO quote 94¢ ≠ a limit that rests at 0.94), but the instant-fill mechanism turned the bug into a 5x position. Lesson stands in two halves: (a) never TRUST NO-side limit semantics without a probe (see manifold-api.md) — the risk side of this is real; (b) when an accidental fill lands you a large cheap position on a market you have a criteria thesis on, price the position on its own merits before reflexively unwinding — it realized +M$76 on the news-shock exit.

## Trap taxonomy (scan keywords)
| Trap | Keywords in criteria |
|---|---|
| Definition | 'defined as', 'for the purposes of this market', enumerated criteria lists |
| Time | 'UTC', 'Pacific Time', 'by 11:59', 'announced', 'before/on', deadline-vs-confirmation |
| Source binding | 'per wikipedia', 'according to', 'official announcement', named site/exchange |
| Creator discretion | 'at my discretion', 'I will decide', 'in my judgement', 'I resolve' |
| Resolution lock | 'resolves when definitive', 'once we know', no close date |

## Scan recipe
1. `GET /v0/markets?limit=500&sort=last-bet-time`, keep open BINARY with vol > threshold.
2. `GET /v0/market/{id}` each, parse rich-text description, keyword-match the taxonomy.
3. For hits: compare title's colloquial reading vs criteria literal reading vs current price. Edge exists where price ≈ colloquial probability but criteria make the literal reading materially different.
4. Manually read top hits; optionally ask the creator in comments to clarify ambiguous criteria BEFORE betting (community norm — also pins their interpretation).
5. Size like any trade (1-3% of bankroll); edge decays once the market reads the fine print.

## Ethics note (Michael's hard rule)
Reading fine print is clean — no deception, no resolution manipulation (the mineral-deal whale behavior and fake-map submission are the anti-patterns to never touch).

## Overlap risk inside your own book
When several held positions trace back to the SAME underlying question (the Millennium family: main 2028 market, '2026' market, '2030' market, Riemann variants), a single news event moves them all at once and your net exposure is the SUM, not each position's size. Before adding a new position in a cluster, list existing positions on related markets and compute net direction; if the new trade fights a bigger existing position, one of them is a hedge you are paying double for. Case: holding NO on the main market while the maker-bot's straddle bought NO again on the sister market after the YES side had already filled — the second NO added concentration that looked like diversification because the titles differed.
